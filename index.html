What is a Combination?

A combination is a selection from a group of items without regard to their order.

nCr = n! / [ r! (n − r)! ]
n = total number of objects in the set
r = number of objects selected
Read as “n choose r”. Also written C(n,r) or Cᵣⁿ.

Remember: n! (factorial) means n × (n−1) × … × 1. Also, 0! = 1.

Why does order not matter?

Players 1, 2, 3: how many teams of two?

1-2 = 2-1
1-3 = 3-1
2-3 = 3-2
Team 1-2 is the same team as 2-1. So we count each team once.

3C2 = 3!/(2!·1!) = 3 teams

Same idea: letters B,E,A,U,Y — A-U-B-E and E-A-U-B are one combination.

Permutation vs Combination

Take 2 out of 4 boxes: A, B, C, D

Permutation: 12 (order matters)
ABBAACCAADDABCCBBDDBCDDC
Combination: 6 (order doesn’t matter)
AB=BAAC=CAAD=DABC=CBBD=DBCD=DC

Each combination is counted r! times in permutations, so nCr = nPr ÷ r! → 12 ÷ 2 = 6.

Lock code 1-2-3 ≠ 3-2-1 (permutation). Pizza toppings or committee members (combination).

Evaluating nCr: Step by Step

Example: 8C4 → n = 8, r = 4
8C4 = 8! / [4!(8−4)!] = 8! / (4!·4!)
= (8·7·6·5·4!) / (4!·4!) = (8·7·6·5) / (4·3·2·1) Cancel the biggest factorial first!
= 1680 / 24 = 70
More: 5C2 = 10 · 3C3 = 1 · 10C3 = 120 · 7C4 = 35

Word Problem: Choosing from One Group

How many ways can 3 blouses be chosen from 10 blouses?

10C3 = 10!/[3!·7!] = (10·9·8)/(3·2·1) = 720/6 = 120

Why combination? Picking blouses A, B, C is the same as C, B, A.

Check yourself: 12C9 = 12C3 = 220 (using nCr = nC(n−r))

Choosing from Several Groups

A basket: 4 star apples, 5 mangoes, 8 guavas. Choose 2 apples, 1 mango, 2 guavas.

Use the Fundamental Counting Principle: multiply the combinations of each group.
4C2 · 5C1 · 8C2 = 6 · 5 · 28 = 840 ways

Committee: 4 men from 12 and 4 women from 9:
12C4 · 9C4 = 495 · 126 = 62,370

“At Least” Problems

Committee of 3 from 6 women and 5 men, with at least 2 women.

Split into cases, then add:
2 women, 1 man: 6C2 · 5C1 = 15 · 5 = 75
3 women, 0 men: 6C3 · 5C0 = 20 · 1 = 20
75 + 20 = 95 committees

Tip: “and” → multiply · “or” (different cases) → add.

Quick Check: P or C?

Summary

  • Combination: order does not matter. Permutation: order matters.
  • Formula: nCr = n! / [r!(n−r)!]
  • Multiple groups → multiply; separate cases → add.
  • Ask: “If I rearrange the chosen items, is it a different result?” Yes → permutation. No → combination.
